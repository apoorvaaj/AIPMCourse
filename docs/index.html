AI Product Management Programme

How to decide, specify, test and launch products built on artificial intelligence

  • 14 weeks
  • Week 0 + 12 modules
  • 3 terms + capstone
  • 6–8 hours per week
  • No coding required
  • Updated 21 Oct 2026

This programme teaches the decisions that make or break products built on artificial intelligence (AI): whether to use AI at all, how to specify behaviour that varies, how to test it, how to pay for it, how to govern it, and how to defend a launch decision. It follows the structure of leading public courses and runs every assignment on one case company, a fictional Singapore polyclinic group, so that learners from any background work on the same realistic problems.

2. The running case: Harbourview Polyclinics

Case company · fictional

Harbourview Polyclinics

Harbourview is a fictional group of public polyclinics (primary care clinics) in Singapore. It offers general practice, chronic disease care, vaccinations, health screening and an on-site pharmacy at each clinic. Patients book, check queue status and see their medicines in the Harbourview app. Any resemblance to a real organisation is unintended.

9clinics across the island
1.4Mpatient visits a year
~2,100staff, 260 doctors
38%of visits for chronic conditions
11%of booked appointments missed

The board has asked the new head of digital products to build an AI portfolio within 18 months, under a tight budget and close scrutiny from the Ministry of Health. Five initiatives are on the table. Each module uses one or more of them.

InitiativeWhat it would doKind of AIMain tension
A. Medicine questions assistantAnswers patients' questions about their own prescribed medicines in the appLarge language model (LLM) with retrieval from approved leafletsPatient safety versus convenience
B. Booking and symptom-routing chatbotHelps patients book the right service and flags urgent symptoms for a nurseLLM with rules and human handoffCatching emergencies versus over-referral
C. Consultation note drafterDrafts the doctor's visit notes from the recorded consultationSpeech-to-text plus LLMDoctor time saved versus errors in the medical record
D. Missed-appointment predictorPredicts who will miss an appointment so staff can send reminders or overbookClassical machine learning (ML)Efficiency versus fairness to some patient groups
E. Patient feedback analyserGroups thousands of free-text comments into themes for clinic managersLLM classificationSpeed versus missing serious complaints

Each module adds a case pack with the extra material needed: interview notes, sample outputs, cost figures, policy extracts. If you are already working on an AI product of your own, you may use it for any assignment instead, as long as it raises the same questions.

3. How assessment works

Every module has two kinds of assessment. Formative assessment checks understanding while you learn. It gives instant feedback, can be retaken, and does not count toward your grade. Summative assessment at the end of each module measures what you can do once the module is over, and it is graded.

Rules that link the two

  • The end-of-module test opens only after both checkpoint quizzes have reached mastery.
  • Module mark bands: Distinction 75% and above, Merit 60–74%, Pass 50–59%, Resubmit below 50%. One resubmission is allowed per module, capped at Pass.
  • To complete the programme: Merit or better on at least 9 of the 11 taught modules, and a passed capstone defence.
  • The programme is self-paced. You email your Week 0 baseline and each module's deliverable to the course team (course email) from the module page. The next module opens once you have done this and reached mastery on both checkpoint quizzes. The course team replies to arrange your end-of-module test and viva.
  • Answers and marking guides for end-of-module tests are kept in a separate assessor pack and never appear in learner pages.

6. Marking rubric for deliverables

Each criterion is scored 0–4 and weighted equally. The total is scaled to the 60% deliverable weight.

Criterion4 · Distinction3 · Merit2 · Pass
Decision qualityClear recommendation; alternatives and trade-offs quantifiedClear recommendation; alternatives namedRecommendation present but weakly argued
EvidenceCase data, evaluation results or cost models drive the conclusion; assumptions labelledEvidence present but partly assumedMostly assertion
Risk handlingFailure modes, who is harmed, mitigations and remaining risk namedMain risks named and mitigatedRisks listed only
CommunicationAn executive can act on it after five minutesClear but longHard to follow

A score of 0–1 on any criterion means the deliverable goes back for a rewrite before it is graded.

7. Access and your data

You sign in with your email address. Each time, the site sends a one-time code to your inbox, so there is no password to create, remember or leak. The programme stores only two things: your email address and your quiz and assignment progress. It does not ask for your name, employer or anything else. You can ask for your account and progress to be deleted at any time. A full privacy notice, written to meet Singapore's Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA), will be published before the programme opens.
